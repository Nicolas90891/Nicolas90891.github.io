What Does Progress Mean for Everyday Life?

A wider international perspective on development and the people it should serve.

What does a country’s progress mean for the people who live it?

Japan and Morocco offer a revealing lens through which to examine how countries develop, how institutions function, and how national choices shape everyday life.

Drawing on experience gained through living, working and travelling across many countries, I have chosen these two nations because of the striking contrasts and lasting impact I encountered in both. That wider international perspective will inform the discussion throughout.

Politics, justice, economy, society and culture are starting points—not boundaries. The series will also explore health, education, business and other interconnected issues, examining how systems work in practice and what they mean for people’s wellbeing, opportunities and futures.

Documented information, firsthand observations and relevant personal experiences will bring these questions into focus: achievements worth understanding, shortcomings that demand attention, and lessons with significance beyond either country.
