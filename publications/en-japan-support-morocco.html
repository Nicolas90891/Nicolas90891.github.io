Japan’s support. Morocco’s opportunity. The people’s right to results.

The figures presented in this graphic cover ten years, 2016–2025, using a conversion of US$1 = ¥105:

Grants: US$36.7 million — averaging US$3.67 million annually.

Concessional loans: US$1.31 billion — averaging US$131 million annually.

Technical cooperation: US$57.6 million — averaging US$5.76 million annually.

Approximate total: US$1.404 billion.

Annual average: US$140.4 million.

Japan’s cooperation brings financial resources, expertise and training to projects involving water, infrastructure, education, healthcare and local development. These contributions deserve recognition, and their outcomes deserve public scrutiny.

Grants, loans and technical cooperation serve different purposes. Loans must be repaid. Infrastructure needs maintenance. Training should build lasting local capabilities.

What does the Moroccan public gain?

Moroccan governments must explain how priorities are chosen, which communities benefit, what projects deliver and how results are measured. Citizens deserve accessible information about both the benefits and the financial obligations undertaken in their name.

For comparison, this Japanese graphic covers ten years, while the European graphic covers five. Annual averages help account for the different periods; a full comparison also requires matching definitions and distinguishing commitments from disbursements.

Gratitude and accountability belong together.

Respect for Japan’s contribution means taking its purpose seriously.

Respect for the Moroccan people means demanding tangible improvements in their lives.
