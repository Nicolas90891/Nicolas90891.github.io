The Requests Are on the Table. Where Are the Results?

The value of cooperation should be visible beyond the conference hall.

By Nicolas Furukawa

The requests are on the table. Where are the results?

Wells, equipment, assistance for underserved communities—and another round of support. This caricature asks a serious question behind the diplomatic smiles: what does international cooperation actually deliver to ordinary people?

Partnerships should build lasting capacity, improve public services and create opportunities. Their value should be visible beyond conference halls—in reliable access to water, functioning schools, accessible healthcare and work that offers a future.

Asking for support also means answering questions. Who sets the priorities? How are resources used? Who benefits—and who checks the results?

International cooperation deserves public accountability.
