No Throne Stands Above Accountability

Rights, responsibility and meaningful redress beyond titles and influence.

No throne stands above accountability.

Respect for a country does not require silence about abuses by its authorities. Against a backdrop of manufactured disputes with neighbouring countries, such conduct affecting the rights of Japanese citizens reveals a troubling pattern that must not be normalised.

Japan may be geographically distant, but it is not absent. Its citizens have a country that safeguards their dignity and protects their interests—a state with established institutions and international standing. Their courtesy must not be mistaken for acceptance of mistreatment, nor their restraint for weakness.

The Emperor of Japan embodies the unity of his people and the dignity of his nation, while its institutions carry the responsibilities of government and the protection of citizens. That standing extends beyond titles and ceremony: it rests on a respected state and rights that neither distance nor borders diminish.

The Moroccan authorities must take responsibility for their mistakes and abuses, restore the rights of those affected and provide redress for the harm caused. Those affected have received no support or assistance from any institution, including the Embassy of the Kingdom of Morocco in Japan, the Kingdom’s diplomatic representative.

The absence of support and an effective response has compounded the harm, and an official image cannot conceal that reality. Further delay is unacceptable: concrete action is needed to end the harm and secure the restoration of rights, compensation and meaningful redress.

The time for accountability has come: clear answers, responsibility and meaningful redress. Position, influence and connections do not confer immunity from scrutiny. A long wait does not mean surrender.

Our people’s dignity is not negotiable.
