Between Sunrise & Sunset

Japan and Morocco — Two Nations, Two Paths

Looking beyond the skyline to institutions, opportunities and everyday life.

By Nicolas Furukawa

A tower can transform a city’s skyline. But what transforms the lives of the people beneath it?

Major projects capture our attention, as do the promises of development. Yet progress takes on a deeper meaning when we look at everyday life: at schools, hospitals and workplaces, and at how institutions treat someone seeking a service or defending a right. Between the public image and the lived experience lies the starting point for Between Sunrise & Sunset.

Why Japan and Morocco?

Living, working and travelling across many countries, alongside more than twenty years in international projects involving architecture and infrastructure, has exposed me to different approaches to planning, management and human relationships. I have chosen Japan and Morocco because of the lasting impact each has had on my experience, and because their contrasts raise questions that reach beyond either country to the meaning of development and the quality of institutions.

In Japan, the ability to combine technical knowledge, organisation and attention to detail deserves close examination, especially when these qualities become part of services and daily life. In Morocco, considerable potential and the contradictions I have encountered raise questions about priorities, the allocation of resources and accountability. We will explore how these outcomes emerge and what can be learned from them, while respecting both peoples, their histories and their cultures.

Two towers, a wider conversation

Tokyo Skytree, standing 634 metres tall, and the 250-metre Mohammed VI Tower will provide a visual entry point into the series. Their functions and contexts differ, so the comparison extends beyond height and appearance to planning, design, construction, the relationship with the surrounding city and the experience offered to the public. What gets built, and how it is delivered and operated, can reveal much about the priorities behind it.

At Tokyo Skytree, engineering complexity, seismic conditions, urban integration and care for visitors open a discussion about the relationship between technology and service. They also offer a way to explore omotenashi, thoughtful hospitality, and kikubari, anticipating the needs of others, and how such values become practical details experienced by the user.

My firsthand observations of the Mohammed VI Tower raise questions about design, materials, finishes, management and maintenance, as well as its surroundings, accessibility and prices relative to purchasing power. These observations and their supporting information will be presented with a clear distinction between evaluating the project experience and assessing structural safety. The question connecting these dimensions is what value the investment returns to society.

From projects to institutions

Behind every project are institutions that choose, decide, manage resources and bear responsibility for results. We will move from architecture to the structure of power, the constitutional roles of Japan’s Emperor and Morocco’s King, and their respective relationships with institutions and accountability. We will then consider how policy and administration shape justice, the economy and public services, and how decisions create or restrict opportunities.

This perspective also encompasses a country’s relationship with its citizens and local communities, its regional neighbours and its international partners. How do domestic and foreign choices affect people’s interests, security and future? How do reputation and international relationships influence employment, investment and trust? These connections matter because people’s lives are shaped both by what happens within their country and by how it engages with the world.

People’s lives as the measure

Politics, justice, the economy, society and culture open the conversation. The series will also explore health, education, work, rights, freedoms and other connected issues. A discussion about a building may lead to the distribution of opportunities; a question about treatment may lead to cost, dignity and trust in institutions. The subjects will develop as the series progresses and these connections come into view.

What matters is understanding what people can actually access: education that opens possibilities, treatment within reach, work that offers stability and redress when harm occurs. When promises and outcomes diverge, accountability becomes essential. Who benefits from decisions? Who bears the cost when they fail? And what allows a change of course?

International experience, grounded discussion

My professional and personal experience will contribute where it helps explain an issue or illuminate a question, within a perspective shaped by different countries and environments. I will draw on documented information, firsthand observations and relevant experiences, distinguishing what sources establish from my own assessment so that readers can follow and question the argument on a clear basis.

The aim is to look beyond first impressions towards causes and consequences. What can we learn from a success worth understanding? What does repeated failure reveal about how institutions work? A rich history and an impressive skyline gain deeper meaning when we examine their impact on rights, services and opportunities, and on people’s ability to build a future they can trust.

A weekly invitation to look closer

Fifteen weekly episodes, coming soon across multiple platforms, with support from professional teams specialising in editorial development and production. The journey begins with Japan and Morocco and remains open to wider experiences and questions, connecting achievements, contradictions and the search for development that serves people.

Follow Between Sunrise & Sunset and join the conversation: where do you see the clearest gap between the image of progress and the reality of everyday life?
