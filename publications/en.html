Between Sunrise & Sunset

Japan and Morocco in comparison. People’s lives at the heart of it.

A tower catches the eye. An agreement carries promises. A title opens doors. But what do these images mean to someone seeking an opportunity, a service that respects their dignity, or redress for a wrong?

The journey begins in the distance between what is proclaimed and what people experience.

Two countries in focus. A wider world of experience.

The series places Japan and Morocco at the centre of the comparison, drawing on examples from more than twenty years of international projects and of working, living and travelling across many countries.

Architecture and infrastructure lead us to the choices that shape cities and societies. How a project is planned, delivered and managed—and how people experience it—opens broader questions about priorities, competence, culture and the value of investment.

The Emperor of Japan and the King of Morocco: roles, institutions and accountability

What place does each hold within the constitutional system? How are powers and responsibilities distributed among the head of state, government, parliament and judiciary? The comparison examines their different roles, then follows the relationship between constitutional provisions and practice: who makes decisions, who can review them, and who answers for the consequences?

These questions extend to ministries, public administration and embassies. How do they respond when a citizen needs protection, a service or redress? What avenues remain when that response falters or never comes?

From agreements to everyday life

Partnerships are announced, resources allocated and development promised. Then comes the test of results: what reaches schools, hospitals and workplaces? Who gains an opportunity, and who remains excluded?

The series connects economics and international cooperation with justice, health, education, rights and freedoms. It explores how the media and society expose failings and follow their correction. Values and human relationships remain part of the picture: everyday encounters reveal what respect, trust and responsibility mean in practice.

Open files. Questions that waiting will not erase.

The published articles have raised questions about trust behind titles, the returns on international support, and citizens’ rights when responses are delayed. The series will pursue these themes, bringing together seemingly separate facts to reveal their connections and consequences.

In examining the Moroccan experience, it will return to practices that call for explanation and harm that still awaits remedy. Statements will be read alongside decisions, and promises alongside what was delivered, with responses and subsequent action presented together. Established facts will be distinguished from personal assessment, and failures from suspected misconduct and what the evidence supports.

Unanswered questions will remain under scrutiny. What has been published so far is the beginning of what deserves to be known.

Fifteen episodes. A picture coming into focus.

A journey grounded in documented information, direct observations and international experience—to understand achievements, examine contradictions and explore what can change.

Coming soon, one episode each week. Follow Between Sunrise & Sunset: the closer we look at the details, the more our understanding of the picture evolves.
