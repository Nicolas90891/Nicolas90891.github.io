Japan is not alone… so what reaches the people?

Following our discussion of Japanese support, we now turn to European support for Morocco. Japan, the European Union and other partners provide grants, financing and expertise to support development and improve people’s lives.

Over five years, 2021–2025, the EU bilateral allocations and European Investment Bank financing shown in this graphic total approximately US$4.02 billion—an annual average of approximately US$803 million. These figures cover allocations and signed financing commitments; they are not all grants or funds already disbursed.

Yet in July 2026, approximately 80,000 people crossed from Morocco into Ceuta, with hundreds more entering Melilla, according to the European Parliament. Parliament condemned the instrumentalisation of migration and called on Morocco to honour its commitments to its partners.

Why, after years of international support, do so many still see their future beyond the border?

Moroccan governments must answer for the persistence of poverty, deprivation and unequal access to opportunity. They must explain how resources are used, what projects deliver and how public decisions improve everyday life.

Allegations that authorities facilitated crossings or exploited migrants to exert political pressure deserve transparent investigation and accountability wherever responsibility is established. Partnership also demands respect for neighbouring countries.

People deserve a future in their own country.

Their dignity must never become a bargaining chip.
